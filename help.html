Get Help

Asking for help is a strong move, not a weak one. These options are free and private.

Emergency: call 911 now Call 911 immediately if a person is very hard to wake or unresponsive, has trouble breathing, has a seizure, chest pain, or is in danger. Stay with them if it is safe, and do not wait to see if it passes.

Other places to start

School guidance counselor

Free, on campus and used to helping students. They can listen and point you to the right services.

Doctor or health center

A doctor or your barangay health center can assess what is going on and refer you to treatment and rehabilitation services.

A trusted person

A parent, relative, teacher, coach or older sibling. You can start with one sentence.

Worried about a friend?

Signs that may suggest a problem, especially when several appear together and last:

  • Sudden changes in mood, friends, grades or attendance
  • Secretive behavior, losing interest in hobbies, asking for money
  • Changes in sleep, appetite or appearance

These signs can have other causes, so do not accuse. Instead:

  1. Talk privately and start with care: "I'm worried about you, and I'm here for you."
  2. Listen without judging or lecturing.
  3. Encourage help from a counselor, trusted adult or doctor, and offer to go with them.
  4. Do not keep safety risks secret. If their life may be in danger, tell a trusted adult or call for help right away.
Words to try "I'm not here to judge. I've noticed some changes, and I care about you. Can we talk to someone together?"
