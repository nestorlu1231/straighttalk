The Law

A short, student-friendly overview of the Philippines' main drug law and who does what.

Republic Act 9165

The Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act of 2002 (Republic Act No. 9165) is the main Philippine law on dangerous drugs. It was signed on June 7, 2002 and took effect on June 22, 2002, replacing the older Dangerous Drugs Act of 1972 (RA 6425).

The law deals with illegal drugs and the chemicals used to make them. It is meant to cover both stopping the drug trade and responding to drug use through prevention, treatment and rehabilitation.

Who does what?

Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB)

The policy-making and strategy-formulating body for drug prevention and control. It can also update the list of substances considered dangerous drugs.

Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA)

The implementing arm of the DDB. It enforces the law against illegal drugs and controlled chemicals.

The Department of Health also plays a role in treatment and rehabilitation, and schools take part in prevention and education.

Consequences go beyond the law

  • Health: harm to the brain and body, overdose, and the risk of dependence.
  • School: falling grades, discipline, or losing your place and scholarships.
  • Future: a record can affect jobs, travel and opportunities.
  • Relationships: trust with family and friends can be damaged.
  • Safety: unknown pills and powders may contain dangerous substances, and dealers put people at risk.
Law details change This page is a simple overview, not legal advice. For the exact provisions and any updates, check official sources such as the Dangerous Drugs Board and PDEA, or ask a lawyer.
